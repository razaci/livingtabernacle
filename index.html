The concept

The Tabernacle is the game's symbolic CPU: the outer court receives sensory input, the laver filters signals, the lampstand allocates attention, the incense altar manages emotional priorities, the inner sanctuary protects memory, and the Ark integrates decisions. The surrounding tents represent traits and habits, each competing for limited attention and energy. These are creative analogies, not literal anatomical or medical claims.

Solo

Restore the six processing stations before the disruption meter reaches 12.

Versus

Reduce the rival Camp Integrity from 20 to 0, or control all six stations at the end of your turn.

Session

30–60 minutes · recommended deck 30 cards per player; use all 90 as a shared collector pool.

What you need

The 90-card collector's pool, 6 station markers (paper is fine), counters for Energy, Focus, Connection, and Disruption, plus a discard pile. For versus play, build two different 30-card decks from the 90-card pool; remaining cards are not used that match. For solo play, build a 30-card deck and use a separate 12-step Disruption track.

Setup

  1. Shuffle your 30-card deck and draw five cards.
  2. Set Energy to 5 (maximum 10), Focus to 0, Connection to 0, and Camp Integrity to 20.
  3. Place the six stations in a row: Gate, Laver, Lampstand, Incense, Memory, Ark.
  4. Each station begins inactive. Place your Camp area beside the stations, with space for up to five creatures.
  5. For solo mode, set Disruption to 0 and prepare the 12-step track. For versus, each player starts with 20 Integrity.

Each turn

  1. Refresh: Ready exhausted creatures and clear effects marked “until next turn.”
  2. Draw: Draw one card; if your deck is empty, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck.
  3. Charge: Gain 3 Energy, to a maximum of 10.
  4. Deploy: Play at most one creature by paying its printed Energy cost. A creature enters exhausted and cannot attack until your next turn.
  5. Process: Activate one ready creature ability, or activate a station by paying 2 Focus plus 1 Connection.
  6. Battle: Each ready creature may attack once. The defender can assign one ready creature to block. Compare Attack with Defense; if Attack exceeds Defense, deal the difference to the defender's Integrity. Unblocked attacks deal 2 Integrity damage instead. Exhaust attackers and blockers.
  7. End: In solo mode, add 1 Disruption unless you activated a station this turn; discard to seven cards.

Card text wins: If an illustrated card's specific effect differs from these general rules, use the printed effect where possible. If a card refers to a resource not otherwise defined, treat it as a thematic keyword and use the glossary below.

Each station can be activated only once per game, unless a card explicitly resets it. Activating a station marks it restored and grants its bonus.

Resources and effects

Energy Pays to deploy cards. Focus Represents attention and is gained when a ready creature uses its action to Focus (+1), instead of using its printed ability. Connection Represents linked processing and is gained when two creatures of different families are ready in your Camp at the start of Process (+1, once per turn). Integrity Camp health, maximum 20. Disruption Solo pressure clock.

Fear, Panic, Stress, Fatigue, Confusion, Overload, Corruption: These are negative effects. Place a marker on a creature when a card inflicts one; a creature with any negative effect has −1 Attack until cleansed (not cumulative). Healing / restoration: Restore Integrity or the specified stat, up to its printed starting value. Shields: Prevent the next 2 damage. Regeneration: Restore 1 Integrity at the start of the owner's next turn. Debuff: Any negative effect.

Winning

Solo: Activate all six stations before Disruption reaches 12. If Disruption reaches 12 first, the Camp collapses and you lose. Versus: Win by reducing opposing Integrity to 0, or by activating all six stations and maintaining control until your next turn begins. A player cannot activate a station already activated by the other player; place separate station rows for versus play.

Card 89 and the finale

89 — Hippocampal Phoenix: Memory Rekindle returns one discarded ally to hand or restores 3 Energy to an ally and gains one Connection token. 90 — Crown of Coherence: Coherence Ascension, once per game, removes negative effects from your Camp and grants +2 Attack and +2 Defense for three turns while preventing Debuffs. In the collector's narrative, the Crown represents completion of the mind-machine.

Example turn

You begin with 5 Energy, two ready creatures of different families, and one Focus. Draw a card, charge to 8 Energy, deploy a 3-Energy creature (5 remain), gain 1 Connection for the two ready families, and use a ready creature to Focus (+1 Focus). You now have 2 Focus and 1 Connection: spend both to activate the Laver, remove a negative effect, and mark that station restored. Then attack with any remaining ready creature.

Collector and printing notes

The archive includes the previously assembled 90-card reference gallery and its 90 small panel crops, plus recoverable cinematic art files. These resources are not all verified one-to-one against the previously illustrated numbered creatures; the original filenames are preserved to avoid falsely matching an image to a card. The final 89 artwork is included at full size; the 90 preview is a crop from a six-card concept panel, not a standalone print-quality illustration. Before ordering physical cards, proofread text and use the printer's official bleed and safe-area template.
